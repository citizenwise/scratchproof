Privacy

Last updated: 6 November 2026

Who we are. ScratchProof is made by Mayank Singh Kunwar (individual developer), 111, Vasudevpuram, RTO Road, Haldwani, Uttarakhand 263139, India, trading as Citizenwise. Contact: support@citizenwise.app.

In short. No account, no analytics, no ads, no tracking. Your photos, videos, damage marks, locations, bills and letters stay on your phone. We have no server that receives them.

What stays on your phone

Everything you capture or type in the app is encrypted on your phone (AES-256-GCM). One exception: the photos and damage notes of an unfinished pickup are kept unencrypted on the phone, inside the app and left out of backups, until you finish or discard that pickup; then they are encrypted or deleted. The key is kept in the phone's secure storage (iOS Keychain or Android Keystore). On iPhone, an encrypted iCloud or computer backup can carry the key to your next iPhone. Nothing leaves the phone unless you share it yourself, for example a Dispute Pack PDF, a backup file or a photo zip. "Share photos (zip)" on a trip makes a copy of that trip's original photos and a README with its proof codes; this copy is not encrypted, so anyone you send it to can open it. The app checks each photo against its seal, writes the zip to its own temporary folder, hands it to your phone's share sheet, and it is deleted from the phone after sharing (on Android after two minutes, so a mail app can finish reading it).

Location (optional)

The first time you start a pickup or return, the app asks whether to add where the photos were taken. Choose "Skip, use time only" and nothing about your location is read; we ask again at most once every 30 days, and you can switch it on or off at any time under "Location on sealed photos" in the app's Settings. If you allow it, the app reads your location only while you use the app, never in the background: when a capture starts it switches location on briefly to get a quick fix ready (that reading is not kept), and it keeps one reading when you seal a pickup or a return (latitude, longitude, how accurate the phone says it is, the time of the reading, and on Android whether the phone flagged it as a mocked location). That reading is sealed with the photos, encrypted on your phone, and stays on your phone. It is never sent anywhere by the app: there is no map service and no address lookup, and the Dispute Pack prints the coordinates as plain text, with no map. It only leaves the phone inside a Dispute Pack or backup file that you share yourself, or when you tap "Open in maps" on the Dispute Pack screen, which hands the coordinates to your phone's own maps app (on iPhone the link is an Apple Maps link: if the Maps app is not installed, it opens Apple’s maps website, which then receives the coordinates). If you allow only an approximate location, the seal records an approximate area.

Agreement scan (optional)

You can take one photo of your rental agreement to fill in the company, the plate, the agreement number and the return time. The text recognition runs on your phone (Apple's Vision on iPhone; on Android, Google's ML Kit with its model built into the app): nothing is sent, and no model is downloaded. The app switches off ML Kit's usage reports to Google. The recognised text is not kept: only the fields you confirm are saved, and you can change them at any time. The photo of the agreement is encrypted on your phone with your trip, like your other photos; it is not sealed, and it leaves the phone only inside a Dispute Pack or backup file that you share yourself.

What is sent, and to whom

  • Independent timestamp (FreeTSA). When you seal photos, the app sends only the fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash) of the sealed record to the FreeTSA time-stamping service at freetsa.org. A fingerprint cannot be turned back into your photos. FreeTSA sees your internet address, as any website does.
  • Purchases (Apple, Google and RevenueCat). Payments are handled by the App Store or Google Play. We use RevenueCat to check what you bought. RevenueCat receives a random app user ID, your purchase and subscription history from the store, and, when you use a trip pass, a random trip identifier so the pass is not used twice. It does not receive your photos, names, places or bills.
  • Backups you make. "Back up / move to a new phone" makes a file locked with a passphrase you choose. Where you save or send it (Files, Drive, email) is up to you. We cannot open it and cannot recover a forgotten passphrase.

Permissions

Camera: to take the photos, the silent return video and the optional photo of your rental agreement (the app never uses the microphone). Location (optional, only while you use the app): to seal where the photos were taken, as described above. Motion sensors: to take a photo when the phone is steady. Notifications: reminders made on your phone, never sent from a server. You can turn each one off in your phone's settings.

What we do not do

We do not sell or share your data, we have no analytics or advertising tools in the app, and we do not build profiles. The app is not directed at children under 13.

Your choices and rights

Settings > "Delete all data" removes every trip, photo, seal, bill, reminder and setting from your phone, and the encryption key. On iPhone, earlier iCloud or computer backups made before you deleted still contain the encrypted trips and their key until those backups are replaced or deleted. Purchases stay with your App Store or Google Play account and can be restored. Delete the app to delete everything it stored on your phone, too. Either way, delete any backup files or zips you saved elsewhere yourself. For questions or requests about purchase data held by RevenueCat, or any data protection request under the laws where you live (for example the GDPR or UK GDPR), write to support@citizenwise.app. Contact us there for anything about this page; we will post changes here with a new date.
