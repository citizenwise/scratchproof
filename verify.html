Proof check

Check a ScratchProof seal

Drop the proof file from a Dispute Pack (it ends in .scratchproof-proof.json). This page re-computes every seal from the file and checks each timestamp signature against FreeTSA’s certificate.

The check runs in your browser. Nothing is uploaded: this page is not allowed to connect anywhere.

Optional: check the original photos

Drop the original photos from the renter’s phone. Each one is fingerprinted (SHA-256) on this device and ticked off against the sealed list. They are not uploaded.

What a match proves

Root hash matches. The sealed text in the file is exactly the text that was sealed on the phone. Change one character, one photo fingerprint or one time in it, and the hash no longer matches.

Timestamp signature checks. The sealed records existed, unchanged, by the timestamp time, according to an independent timestamp (RFC 3161, from FreeTSA). This page checks the signature against FreeTSA’s certificate, which is built into the page.

Seals are linked. The return and the agent’s answer (a signature or a refusal to sign) each name the pickup’s root hash inside their own sealed text, and neither was stamped before the pickup, so they belong to that pickup.

Times agree. Each seal holds the time the phone gave when it was sealed. When that time is more than 10 minutes from the timestamp time, the page warns: the phone clock was wrong, or the seal was stamped later, and only the timestamp time can be relied on. A seal without a timestamp is a warning too: its time rests on the phone clock alone.

A photo matches. The photo is, byte for byte, the photo that was sealed. An edited, resized or re-sent copy (for example from a messaging app) does not match, even when it looks the same.

What it does not prove

A timestamp does not show when each photo was taken. The photo times and any location inside a seal come from the renter’s phone. A seal shows that the records existed, unchanged, by the timestamp time; it does not show who caused any damage.

FreeTSA is a free service, not a qualified timestamp authority. This page does not check whether FreeTSA’s certificate was revoked. You can repeat the timestamp check with openssl ts -verify and the certificate from freetsa.org.
